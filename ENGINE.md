## Installed configuration and mission separation

[`design-contract.js`](dist/design-contract.js) classifies installed hardware, mission inputs, analysis assumptions and controlled allowances. `designIdentity(h)` returns reproducible `vehicleId`, `missionId` and `analysisId` values. The vehicle record includes physical equipment quantities, geometry, the dry ledger, installed electrical/thermal interfaces and the fabric installation. Orbit, duration, workload, duty, service targets and route allocations evaluate that installation. They do not refill tanks, add converters, change pipe bores, increase fabric ports or expand a radiator. An explicit hardware input or accepted sizing proposal creates a revised installed configuration. Launch-only adapter accounting remains distinct from separated spacecraft mass.

The sized hardware record carries four shared design products:

| Record | Definition and consumers |
| --- | --- |
| `h.architecture` | Selected central-core design and matched compact-backbone/local-radiator candidates from `architecture-trades.js`. Alternative mass and packaging estimates retain the common mission/resource boundary; they do not replace the operational geometry. |
| `h.applicability` | Controlled hardware, mission-life and model limits. `inside`, `outside` and `unassessed` bounds describe applicability, not qualification. |

| `h.serviceArchitecture` | Repeated compute/host units, local memory placement, finite fabric, terminal/gateway interface, Earth-service route and assurance provisions. |

The controlled comparison mission is 550 km circular, 53° inclination and five years, with no continuous-sunlight credit. `seasonalIllumination(h)` samples 25 dates in the first and last mission year using the existing Sun/J₂ geometry. The zero-beta circular eclipse remains the conservative energy-sizing bound. Seasonal samples do not silently reduce installed battery or array requirements, and eccentric-orbit results retain their approximation status. The selected architecture has narrower scale limits than some optional component families; choosing a higher-voltage family does not remove the separate appendage, core, tray or life boundaries.

These loss terms feed the bus and heat ledgers once. The selected thermal architecture is pressurized liquid ammonia with one common pump station, a duty/cold-standby pump pair, two isolatable parallel radiator branches and an accumulator. It is not a pair of independent redundant loops. Hardware mass separates line metal, fittings, pumps, accumulator and fluid from radiator metal and tray cold plates already carried elsewhere. Darcy–Weisbach and minor losses, parallel cold plates/radiator passages and a finite pump curve determine the operating point. Flow velocity, differential/absolute pressure, expansion volume and family bore/pump limits remain explicit screens. Pump curves and constant fluid properties are project estimates. The implemented property span is 260–315 K; pressure and vapor-pressure checks do not substitute for a correlated property model or material/seal qualification. The source register identifies the [NASA ISS thermal-control precedent](https://www.nasa.gov/pdf/473486main_iss_atcs_overview.pdf) and [NIST ammonia phase data](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/cbook.cgi?ID=C7664417&Mask=4), together with their application limits.

## Compute, service route and checkpoint custody

[`service-architecture.js`](dist/service-architecture.js) defines a repeated tray with included host resources, local accelerator memory, a 48 V branch, cold-plate interface and an inter-tray port. Complete terrestrial server references retain their included hosts/NICs once. Extra host modules, external switch hardware, cable and controller mass/power are added only in their separate boundaries. A contiguous replica placement or balanced training shard map tests local device memory and the occupied device count; aggregate memory alone does not prove that a real application supports that partition.

`fabricGbps` is requested useful per-tray worker bandwidth. The hardware model chooses 25/50/100/200/400 Gbit/s physical ports with an 80% usable allocation. Single-stage or bounded leaf–spine topology determines switch/port counts, bisection and routing support. Rates are capped at installed useful capacity, including finite storage-controller interfaces. A request above the maximum remains visible. The 80% factor is a protocol/payload allocation; it is not a NASA resource margin. Source device performance does not qualify flight packaging, radiation response or measured application collectives.

The selected optical modem has a 2 Gbit/s line-rate allocation and at most 1,600 Mbit/s of customer service per direction. Requested relay up/down rates are project service assumptions capped by that interface and the gateway. The relay blackout is `max(relayHandoverS, 35 s)` in each allocated cycle. One installed terminal cannot acquire two independently pointed peers simultaneously, so the model grants no make-before-break service. Hybrid operation uses an acquired direct link during appropriate relay outages. A periodic relay schedule has no receiver ephemeris or guaranteed provider access. Direct optical capacity is a clear-sky calculation; site weather and diversity require separate evidence. Independent S-band command/housekeeping stays outside payload load shedding and customer-data custody.

Training reserves `2 × restartGB` for A/B checkpoints. [`checkpointWriteStep`](dist/data-custody.js) retains the complete slot while the alternate slot is pending. New save requests start only when no prior package is in progress; requests arriving during a write are deferred. The write consumes the installed storage capacity left after incoming customer data. Final-artifact serialization uses the remaining write capacity after checkpoint traffic. A recovery point commits atomically in the model only when the whole pending package has drained. A partial write neither advances `lastCompleteUnits` nor replaces the previous complete slot.

Snapshot fields distinguish cumulative write traffic, bytes still pending, completed saves and the latest committed work: `aiCheckpointWriteGB`, `aiCheckpointPendingGB`, `aiCheckpointCommits`, `aiCheckpointCompleteUnits`, `aiCheckpointDeferred` and `aiCheckpointCommitS`. `aiCheckpointCustodyProjected` marks reduced-order custody estimates. Detailed/chronological integration retains discrete pending-write and commit behavior; a representative-day extrapolation is not a reconstructed commit timeline. This is a nominal storage-custody state machine. Checksums, authenticated software recovery, radiation behavior and interruption recovery are verification requirements, not simulated failure events.

## Guided flight and disposal

`sim.story(id)` returns one lazy chapter with its stable event ID, start, end, and preferred instant. `sim.story()` returns the ordered ten chapters. The first sixty days are integrated chronologically. A subsequent representative-day preview positions later events. A completed mission audit supplies chronological checkpoints for all dates. Short missions can lack a particular operational event. Such a chapter returns its searched interval and reason. Prelaunch integration runs at MET −3600 to −600 seconds, followed by the pad until liftoff. The selected vehicle’s geographic pad rotates with Earth. A spherical direct-ascent screen uses the launch UTC, pad latitude, longitude and requested inclination to compute the candidate insertion plane, heading and insertion state. The kinematic loft reaches that state at MET 540 s. The selected carrier then determines payload release. Conventional carriers release at insertion. The Starship cargo study opens its retained door at MET 540 s, releases at 575 s and clears the spacecraft by 635 s. Release transfers pointing authority to the spacecraft and removes the launch adapter from its mass properties. The launch workspace reports position and plane mismatches for an independently specified orbit. The launch-site change review can adopt site-compatible target angles. Exact imported design angles remain reviewable. The path omits range-safety corridors, doglegs and vehicle trajectory optimization.

The mission chooses `disposalMethod` in the initial design. `sim.disposal()` and `audit.terminal` return the selected route, achieved outcome, actual burns, fuel used, elapsed times, and Earth-fixed ground track. `start` marks first ignition, `cutoff` marks last engine cutoff, and `end` marks surface impact or arrival at the raised orbit. Trapped residuals remain unavailable. Routine reboost protects the selected terminal allocation and the separate maneuver reserve. Failed candidate maneuvers inhibit ignition.

**Raised circular orbit.** Finite prograde thrust near the original perigee raises apogee. A second finite prograde burn is centered on transfer apogee and stops at the desired semimajor axis. The local trial requires both final apsides to lie within 5 km of the target. `burns` contains start/end, role, propellant and delivered Δv for each burn. `finalPerigeeKm` and `finalApogeeKm` are achieved radii minus Earth radius. The vehicle remains in orbit. Orbital lifetime, reentry safety and regulatory compliance are outside this route calculation.

**Targeted reentry.** The default design aims at the fixed South Pacific coordinate 48.9° S, 123.4° W. The solver screens ignition opportunities within the specified final search hours and the last 18% of the mission, estimating the impact phase with two nominal trajectory propagations and searching each intervening ground track. The scheduling estimate includes 65% of initial propellant. The final burn uses actual remaining inventory, finite retrograde thrust and osculating-perigee cutoff. Coordinates outside the orbital latitude envelope are infeasible. A closest executable trajectory can explicitly miss the requested target radius. `targetMet` describes its nominal impact relative to that radius.

**Conditional surface ellipse.** At the descending 120 km interface, `entry`, the spacecraft hardware model stops. A nonlifting, nonablating surviving reference body continues to the spherical surface under central gravity and aerodynamic drag. Relative velocity subtracts the co-rotating atmosphere. The 1976 U.S. Standard Atmosphere geometric-height density samples are log interpolated from sea level to 120 km. The editable ballistic coefficient sets drag. Independent perturbations in entry speed, heading, density scale and ballistic coefficient are propagated to surface. Their symmetric differences supply an east/north covariance matrix in km². Its eigenvalues times −2 ln(0.05) set the squared 95% Gaussian semiaxes. Bearing is clockwise from true north. The ellipse is conditional on survival and the stated uncertainty model. It is distinct from spacecraft-fragment dispersion, a predicted safe zone or a casualty assessment. `entryPoint`, `impact`, `targetErrorKm`, `footprint` and `groundTrack` are available for maps and PDR. After termination, inventories hold the last modeled spacecraft values. `payloadServiceSeconds` and `activeComputeSeconds` in the audit count actual service and processing intervals independently of the terminal trajectory.

The enclosure exchanges heat with the equipment/fluid-loop store through the blanket and modeled seam conductance. The exterior radiative equilibrium receives directional direct light, albedo and Earth IR, averaged over the six bus sides. `enclosureLeakKW` is positive outward. `energyKWh.enclosureOutward` integrates this signed flux. The inner skin, participating frame and installed equipment contribute to `loopCapacityKJK`. `thermalCapacityKJK` reports the combined loop/equipment and radiator capacities; the battery retains its own capacity.

## Thermal state, limits and evidence

`thermal-limits.js` specifies independent operating and survival limits for six modeled nodes. `thermalNodes` maps each component to its temperature and the four scalar design keys. Bounds are assumed engineering requirements and serialize with the design. The survival interval encloses the operating interval. Changes to requirements leave the installed-hardware temperature solution unchanged.

`s.thermalQualification` contains the actual temperature, current component state, activity, applicable band, signed nearest-bound margin and classification. Powered idle and powered computing both use operating limits. The two coolant-side nodes use delivered pump power. An exposed radiator continues in service during eclipse and after a workload stops. Outer insulation switches from a prescribed launch boundary to exposed passive service at separation. Pack activity follows actual charge/discharge power above 1 W. The preflight payload is on ground hold. No observations are accrued after the final propagated state. Separate controls can power-down idle processors, stop a following coolant pump and prescribe the initial core, battery, and launch boundary temperatures.

[`stepThermalNetwork`](dist/thermal-network.js) advances **three finite stores**: the battery, the equipment/fluid loop and the radiator. In the inventory, `packK` is battery temperature, `loopK` is the equipment/mean-fluid temperature, and `heat` is radiator temperature. Snapshots expose these as `batteryK`, `loopK` and `radiatorK`. The loop receives served equipment dissipation, assigned electrical/source-conversion heat and loop cold-control heat. The radiator receives absorbed environmental heat and its heater. Battery conversion loss and its heater enter the battery. The independently applied-heat experiment replaces equipment dissipation while retaining finite heaters, source-conversion effects and battery loss.

The backward-Euler balance uses updated temperatures in both exchange terms and the radiator's fourth-power emission. In kW, seconds and kJ/K, its structure is:

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_\ell\frac{T_\ell^+-T_\ell}{\Delta t}
 &= Q_\ell-Q_{\rm enclosure}+G_b(T_b^+-T_\ell^+)-G_r(T_\ell^+-T_r^+),\\
C_r\frac{T_r^+-T_r}{\Delta t}
 &= Q_r+G_r(T_\ell^+-T_r^+)-k(T_r^+)^4,\\
C_b\frac{T_b^+-T_b}{\Delta t}
 &= Q_b-G_b(T_b^+-T_\ell^+).
\end{aligned}
$$

Here `k` includes the current emitting geometry, coating and space-view terms. A scalar implicit solve resolves radiation after eliminating the linear coupled temperatures. Internal exchanges cancel when the three equations are summed. `thermalResidualKW` reports the discrete energy-balance residual, and `loopToRadiatorKW` reports the actual signed exchange.

The installed battery, loop and radiator heaters have finite ratings and separate thermostat/PWM allocations. Their electrical draw is charged to the power system and their delivered heat goes to the corresponding store. Cold bypass/mixing changes `G_r`, using the allocated loop temperature band, delivered pump support and admitted/deployed radiator branch fraction. It does **not** change physical radiator area, coating properties or absorbed environmental flux. A cold radiator can therefore continue to cool radiatively while the warm loop is bypassed. Inadequate heater power, flow or pressure capacity remains a shortfall; the model adds no unlimited survival power.

`energyTemperatureLadder(h,...)` derives local reported temperatures from the mean loop state and physical transport quantities. With heat transport `Q` in kW, flow `mDot` in kg/s and fluid heat capacity `cp` in J/(kg·K), the fluid rise is `1000 × Q / (mDot × cp)`. Supply and return are placed symmetrically about the mean loop temperature. Per-tray IT power then raises the cold plate above the return fluid through the allocated cold-plate/fluid resistance, and raises the junction above the cold plate through `thermalResistanceKPerKw`. That input is specifically the **per-tray junction-to-cold-plate** resistance. There is no 0.35/0.65 division of a single junction-to-radiator drop. `coolantK` is the modeled return-side value; `loopK` is the mean finite-store value.

Finite temperatures produced by a numerical guard do not establish valid heat transport. `thermalTransportApplicable`, `fluidPropertyInRange`, `liquidPressureMarginPa` and `freezeSeparationK` expose flow, constant-property, liquid-pressure and freezing boundaries. Six component review temperatures are derived from the three integrated stores and the local/enclosure relationships. They are not six independently integrated thermal masses. Temperature requirements are evaluated after the physical temperature solution; a changed allowable does not make hardware run warmer or cooler.

`sim.thermalWindow(start, span)` integrates a selected interval of up to 31 days from the existing trajectory checkpoints. Its rows report the node/state duration in seconds, Tmin and Tmax and their MET, separate hot/cold event counts, seconds and first/last crossings. Its `excursions` lists individual intervals. The model estimates crossing times by linear interpolation of one-sided integration-interval temperatures. Changes of operating state, illumination or workload can produce one-sided extrema. The detailed frame length is at most 60 s. Windows after day 60 use the representative-day initial inventory until a chronological audit is available.

`audit.thermal` has the same summary rows across the integrated mission from liftoff through actual termination. Annual bins also carry thermal rows. Daily records retain the first thermal violation. The legacy field name `thermalQualifiedUntilS` means elapsed time to the first modeled thermal excursion, or the full modeled coverage when none occurred. It is not a hardware-qualification result. `physicalLifetimeSeconds` also considers the other simulated operational constraints and is capped at modeled hardware coverage. A later disposal outcome remains in `firstCrossing` as a separate mission-outcome record; it cannot extend hardware lifetime or become the binding label for an earlier hardware-coverage boundary. The analytic sizing envelope separately evaluates a nominal design point. Supplier limits, uncertainty, test correlation and accepted qualification evidence remain separate PDR gates.

`deriveBound(summary,id,state,side,marginK)` returns the observed extremum and instant, state coverage, provenance, the unconstrained requirement and the proposed compatible threshold. For a minimum the required rating extends down from the observed cold case. For a maximum it extends up from the observed hot case. Neighboring bounds constrain the single solved parameter. Infeasible and unobserved targets have null proposed values. The calculation neither changes installed hardware nor establishes a manufacturer's qualification rating.

`node disposal-engine-test.mjs --five-year` requests the long chronological disposal regression. Results must be read with that run's installed ledger, mission inputs and model revision. Earlier 4.03 t mass and exact disposal-consumption, orbit or footprint examples belonged to an older model and are not current Rev I/model 21.0 reference results. Terminal-dynamics calculations can still be inspected when an equipment rating was exceeded earlier; that does not grant useful service or disposal acceptance to the preceding mission.

## Attitude, articulated power and heat

The mission inventory carries an inertial attitude quaternion, angular rates, wing and radiator encoders, and steerable terminal boresights. Scheduled maneuver preparation, unloading, terminal acquisition and mission phases select the guidance reference. A snapshot exposes both the commanded mode and the achieved pose. Random-access playback propagates from the preceding accepted checkpoint, carrying the body pose, CMG encoders and mechanism momentum together. Terminal pursuit follows the moving Earth station from that checkpoint. The terminal must complete acquisition and lie inside its travel cone and half-beamwidth residual before Ka or optical data can pass. The optical receiver additionally incurs a separate jitter loss. Relay service has an assumed availability schedule without a receiver ephemeris.

Two radiator roots open outward to a clearance cant set by the face-mounted installation (up to 89.96°). After both outer hinges lock, independent span-axis drives trim within ±105° at up to 0.75°/s. The clearance interlock narrows the upper bank’s cant if the rotary sweep could enter the moving terminal envelopes. Each bank’s trim limit also keeps its sheet within its own side of the bank pair. When these limits prevent edge-on pointing, the thermal balance includes the remaining illumination. Sunlit tracking seeks zero direct solar projection. In eclipse a cold loop unwinds the span drive before bringing the root closer to its cold-protection cant. A warm loop reduces Earth view and can prepoint for sunrise in the final 40 seconds of shadow. Cold protection starts 8 K above the higher of the loop and radiator operating minima and releases 5 K warmer. These rates, margins, and the two-axis installation are study choices. Before a chemical burn trim returns to zero before cant returns to ±45°.

Power uses the positive cosine of the achieved photovoltaic normal and solar vector, followed by installed source-conversion and harness limits. Heat rejection uses both sides of the exposed radiator geometry. Direct sunlight heats the Sun-facing side. For planetary loads the solver integrates the absolute projected cosine over the finite Earth disk and divides by π. The corresponding two-face disk factor ranges from 0 to 1. Averaging over the two emitting faces gives an open-space fraction of one minus half this factor. Planetary radiance uses 237 W/m² infrared and an illustrative 0.30 diffuse albedo scaled by the local solar zenith cosine. The closed-form disk integral includes the visible planetary limb for a bank pointing edge-on to Earth. Self-shadowing and panel-to-panel radiation are omitted. Direct, reflected and infrared terms add to absorbed power. The same procedure area-weights the opposing enclosure faces. The battery, loop and radiator stores advance on the mission clock with drive power included, and the local chip/cold-plate ladder follows their current transport state. Coolant admission and bypass affect thermal coupling independently of exposed emitting geometry.

The tracking drive allocation is 12 W plus 24 W per degree/s of the summed absolute representative wing and two radiator-head rates, with the summed rate capped at 2°/s. The allocation describes all four installed wing drives and both radiator drives together. Steerable terminal motion adds 6 W per degree/s, capped at 4°/s. These are engineering assumptions. Motors follow their nominal command profile under the electrical regulation assumption. A hard brownout and subsequent attitude-control recovery are outside the model.

After deployment, the drag model caches exact projected unions of the bus, four wing blankets and two radiator banks in nearest 5° bins for the ram direction and three encoder angles. Gravity-gradient torque uses the current radial vector and full component-based inertia tensor about the vehicle center of mass. Body motion conserves total inertial angular momentum under internal actuation and integrates external torque. Moving appendages and terminal heads contribute their relative angular momentum. Four single-gimbal CMGs provide bounded torque through their current steering Jacobian. Reported stored momentum is the magnitude of their actual vector sum. Chemical ignition checks the actual thrust-axis alignment, configuration-dependent mass and plume clearance, and angular authority to follow the rotating velocity direction. Once admitted, the burn trajectory assumes nominal velocity-vector tracking. The raised-orbit second ignition is screened independently.

After separation, the CMG electrical allocation follows the achieved gimbal-rate duty. The largest absolute gimbal speed sets a cluster duty fraction between zero and one, interpolating the installed idle and maneuver power allocations.

```js
const duty = clamp(maxAbsGimbalRate / Math.max(0.001, gimbalRateLimitRadS), 0, 1);
const cmgKW = (idleW + duty * (maneuverW - idleW)) / 1000;
```

This interpolation is an engineering power estimate. Terminal acquisition and post-contact parking can produce brief CMG power transients. Steady tracking demand follows the ongoing actuator motion and can be higher or lower than the demand immediately before contact. The contact regression samples acquisition, transfer and parking, checks the terminal-induced mechanism momentum, and verifies the gimbal limits, power-law closure and angular-momentum balance.

Flight reference material and the guidance equations are recorded in `dist/attitude-guidance.js`, `dist/attitude-dynamics.js` and `dist/attitude-inertia.js`. NASA's environmental heat formulation provides the one-face nadir disk-factor cross-check and reflected-flux basis at https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20160012100/downloads/20160012100.pdf.

## Preliminary design assessment and export interface

`assessPdrDomains({h,s,day,view,sim,domainIds})` in [`pdr-assessment.js`](dist/pdr-assessment.js) assembles the seventeen domain records for the selected installed vehicle and mission. `domainIds` can select a workspace without performing unrelated domain assessments. Each record retains its original `graphic`, its design/check/evidence products, and any `supplementalGraphics: [{id,title,svg}]`.

The status contract has three axes:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `numericalStatus` / `numericalScope` | Aggregate of assessed numerical comparisons. `meets` means known screens meet; unassessed checks still remain. |
| `applicability.status` | `inside`, `outside` or `unassessed` for explicitly mapped study limits, installed interfaces and model coverage. The record includes the relevant boundary checks and provenance. |
| `evidenceStatus` / `evidence` / `reviewStatus` | Evidence and independent acceptance. These remain open until the corresponding evidence and review decisions exist. |

`screenStatus` remains as a compatibility summary. New consumers should preserve the three axes instead of interpreting any single status as domain closure. `pdrApplicabilityMap` attaches drag-source bounds only to the disciplines using that model, electrical/hydraulic limits to their receiving domains, and fabric/route bounds to the relevant payload/service domains. For example, a requested orbit above the annual drag source range can invalidate propulsion-maintenance sizing while leaving an unchanged compute installation inside its hardware study bounds. A launch-mass shortfall can coexist with an applicable geometry calculation.

[`architecture-trades.js`](dist/architecture-trades.js) supplies the controlled reference and matched alternatives, `structuralBounds(h)`, `navigationAllocation(h)`, `radiationAndLife(h)` and `propulsionUncertaintyBound(h,nominal)`. The mechanical model carries equivalent-beam stiffness bounds, a conservative longeron-only project load case, explicit material-property requirements and factored mechanism torque requirements. They do not establish measured modes, approved material allowables or provider coupled loads. Body sensor/alignment/control/flexible allocations and terminal fine residual are separated. The runtime still uses ideal attitude/state knowledge.

The propulsion sensitivity uses the carried predicted wet mass, 2% lower Isp, 2% maneuver-execution addition, twice the nominal drag-equivalent Δv, the greater of configured trim and the 5 m/s insertion allocation, and a 0.1 m/s navigation allocation. Protected disposal, trapped residual and the 1% gauging allocation are counted once. Operational reserve is available to that sensitivity and is not multiplied again. These are controlled deterministic project allocations; provider/receiver/engine distributions and correlations are still required for the adopted statistical 3σ assessment. Shielding similarly remains an areal/material-path allocation with no dose or SEE probability inferred.

The public references are comparison evidence rather than substitutes for the installed design. Their power boundaries, mission orbits, maturity and launch configurations remain distinct. The mission boundary is one complete operational spacecraft per launch with attached deployment; it receives no external compute, beamed power, external cooling or on-orbit assembly credit. Ground networks and any relay are explicitly external services.

Model scope remains preliminary: the nominal annual drag budget supports circular cases within the 100–900 km source table; flexible structural/control response, detailed slosh and optical jitter are not integrated flight plants; thermal hydraulics use allocated pump curves and bounded constant properties; relay access is a scheduling sensitivity; direct optical weather availability is unassessed; and part-specific radiation, heritage, life and recovery evidence remains open. The mission model represents nominal operation and gradual degradation. Numerical regression, an applicable study envelope, or a favorable simulated service interval does not record hardware qualification or independent PDR acceptance.
