Window and mission return arrays of flat numeric states, events arrays of `{id,type,label,start,end,station?}`. `at(t)` seconds since model epoch returns flat unit-suffixed state. Detailed contact events are `type:'contact'`. Events predictably clipped at requested range only in display, stored start/end times ideally span the physical episode. `mission` is a representative-day reduced-order sweep with periodic checkpoint state, indicated in the output; detailed nearby windows integrate operational states. Model notes enumerate resolution and boundaries.
createSimulation takes sized hardware h from sizeDesign; also accepts flat input via sizeDesign. Chronological verification is available as `await sim.runAudit(({days,totalDays})=>updateProgress(), {signal})`. Its result includes `annual`, `energyKWh`, `eventTotals`, `dayStats`, first operational crossing with slacks, `finalState` and whole-mission extrema. The same sim adopts the resulting daily checkpoints. The solar weather driver accepts `nominal` or `observed`; the latter replays monthly 2009–2019 NRC Canada F10.7 values via NOAA PSL, with monthly interpolation used as the daily proxy and neighboring months as the 81-day proxy. ECSS-E-ST-10-04C Rev.1 Table G-4 supplies the JB-2006 density envelope. Projected states use 90-day representative quadrature with a one-day fast-state spinup and continuous integration across each selected window. All reported lifetime totals preserve cumulative counters; `dayGeneratedGB`, `dayDeliveredGB` etc are named separately. `slewFraction`, `slewTorqueNm`, `slewMomentumNms`, `ramBody` and `orbitLost` are available on live snapshots.

`silhouette.js` builds the projected convex hull of the instrument bus and four wing and two radiator rectangles. It splits the integration line at every projected vertex and edge intersection, unions covered intervals, and tabulates directional samples at 5° for fast repeated evaluations. Partial deployment blends stowed and deployed reference areas. The pressure-center lever remains an editable assumption. The detailed radiator/core path uses one transient heat store with algebraic stage temperatures. RF, clear-sky optical and orbit propagation use the simplifying assumptions enumerated in `modelNotes`.

`textGeneratedGB`, `textDeliveredGB`, `textDroppedGB`, `textQueueGB`, `bulkQueueGB`, `bulkDeliveredGB`, `tokensDelivered`, `textGoodputMbps` and `bulkGoodputMbps` separate generated output text from initial backlog and bulk files. ## Guided flight and disposal

`sim.story(id)` returns one lazy chapter with its stable event ID, start, end, and preferred instant. `sim.story()` returns the ordered ten chapters. The first sixty days are integrated chronologically. A subsequent representative-day preview positions later events. A completed mission audit supplies chronological checkpoints for all dates. Short missions can lack a particular operational event. Such a chapter returns its searched interval and reason. Prelaunch integration runs at MET −3600 to −600 seconds, followed by the pad until liftoff. The selected vehicle’s geographic pad rotates with Earth. A spherical direct-ascent screen uses the launch UTC, pad latitude, longitude and requested inclination to compute the candidate insertion plane, heading and release state. The kinematic loft reaches that state at MET 540 s. The launch workspace reports position and plane mismatches for an independently specified orbit. The launch-site change review can adopt site-compatible target angles. Exact imported design angles remain reviewable. The path omits range-safety corridors, doglegs and vehicle trajectory optimization.

The mission chooses `disposalMethod` in the initial design. `sim.disposal()` and `audit.terminal` return the selected route, achieved outcome, actual burns, fuel used, elapsed times, and Earth-fixed ground track. `start` marks first ignition, `cutoff` marks last engine cutoff, and `end` marks surface impact or arrival at the raised orbit. Trapped residuals remain unavailable. Routine reboost protects the selected terminal allocation and the separate maneuver reserve. Failed candidate maneuvers inhibit ignition.

**Raised circular orbit.** Finite prograde thrust near the original perigee raises apogee. A second finite prograde burn is centered on transfer apogee and stops at the desired semimajor axis. The local trial requires both final apsides to lie within 5 km of the target. `burns` contains start/end, role, propellant and delivered Δv for each burn. `finalPerigeeKm` and `finalApogeeKm` are achieved radii minus Earth radius. The vehicle remains in orbit. Orbital lifetime, reentry safety and regulatory compliance are outside this route calculation.

**Targeted reentry.** The default design aims at the fixed South Pacific coordinate 48.9° S, 123.4° W. The solver screens ignition opportunities within the specified final search hours and the last 18% of the mission, estimating the impact phase with two nominal trajectory propagations and searching each intervening ground track. The scheduling estimate includes 65% of initial propellant. The final burn uses actual remaining inventory, finite retrograde thrust and osculating-perigee cutoff. Coordinates outside the orbital latitude envelope are infeasible. A closest executable trajectory can explicitly miss the requested target radius. `targetMet` describes its nominal impact relative to that radius.

**Conditional surface ellipse.** At the descending 120 km interface, `entry`, the spacecraft hardware model stops. A nonlifting, nonablating surviving reference body continues to the spherical surface under central gravity and aerodynamic drag. Relative velocity subtracts the co-rotating atmosphere. The 1976 U.S. Standard Atmosphere geometric-height density samples are log interpolated from sea level to 120 km. The editable ballistic coefficient sets drag. Independent perturbations in entry speed, heading, density scale and ballistic coefficient are propagated to surface. Their symmetric differences supply an east/north covariance matrix in km². Its eigenvalues times −2 ln(0.05) set the squared 95% Gaussian semiaxes. Bearing is clockwise from true north. The ellipse is conditional on survival and the stated uncertainty model. It is distinct from spacecraft-fragment dispersion, a predicted safe zone or a casualty assessment. `entryPoint`, `impact`, `targetErrorKm`, `footprint` and `groundTrack` are available for maps and PDR. After termination, inventories hold the last modeled spacecraft values. `payloadServiceSeconds` and `activeComputeSeconds` in the audit count actual service and processing intervals independently of the terminal trajectory.

The enclosure exchanges heat with the shared thermal node through the blanket and modeled seam conductance. The exterior radiative equilibrium receives directional direct light, albedo and Earth IR, averaged over the six bus sides. `enclosureLeakKW` is positive outward. `energyKWh.enclosureOutward` integrates this signed flux. The inner skin and participating frame add to `thermalCapacityKJK`.

## Thermal qualification

`thermal-limits.js` specifies independent operating and survival limits for six modeled nodes. `thermalNodes` maps each component to its temperature and the four scalar design keys. Bounds are assumed engineering requirements and serialize with the design. The survival interval encloses the operating interval. Changes to requirements leave the installed-hardware temperature solution unchanged.

`s.thermalQualification` contains the actual temperature, current component state, activity, applicable band, signed nearest-bound margin and classification. Powered idle and powered computing both use operating limits. The two coolant-side nodes use delivered pump power. An exposed radiator continues in service during eclipse and after a workload stops. Outer insulation switches from a prescribed launch boundary to exposed passive service at separation. Pack activity follows actual charge/discharge power above 1 W. The preflight payload is on ground hold. No observations are accrued after the final propagated state. Separate controls can power-down idle processors, stop a following coolant pump and prescribe the initial core, battery, and launch boundary temperatures.

The radiator/store node receives the served bus dissipation or independently applied heat plus the signed heat flow from the battery. The battery has its own thermal mass, electrical conversion losses and conductive link to the loop. A coupled backward Euler step conserves battery-to-loop exchange. Junction, cold-plate and modeled circulating-fluid gradients are fractions of the per-tray conductive drop from the powered IT load or applied-heat experiment. Temperature limits enter evaluation after temperatures are calculated.

`sim.thermalWindow(start, span)` integrates a selected interval of up to 31 days from the existing trajectory checkpoints. Its rows report the node/state duration in seconds, Tmin and Tmax and their MET, separate hot/cold event counts, seconds and first/last crossings. Its `excursions` lists individual intervals. The model estimates crossing times by linear interpolation of one-sided integration-interval temperatures. Changes of operating state, illumination or workload can produce one-sided extrema. The detailed frame length is at most 60 s. Windows after day 60 use the representative-day initial inventory until a chronological audit is available.

`audit.thermal` has the same summary rows across the integrated mission from liftoff through actual termination. Annual bins also carry thermal rows. Daily records retain the first thermal violation. `thermalQualifiedUntilS` is the elapsed time to the first thermal excursion, or the full coverage when none occurred. `physicalLifetimeSeconds` also considers the other simulated operational constraints. The analytic sizing envelope separately evaluates a nominal design point.

`deriveBound(summary,id,state,side,marginK)` returns the observed extremum and instant, state coverage, provenance, the unconstrained requirement and the proposed compatible threshold. For a minimum the required rating extends down from the observed cold case. For a maximum it extends up from the observed hot case. Neighboring bounds constrain the single solved parameter. Infeasible and unobserved targets have null proposed values. The calculation neither changes installed hardware nor establishes a manufacturer's qualification rating.

Run `node disposal-engine-test.mjs --five-year` to repeat both reference-mission chronological disposal checks with the selected reference launch envelope. The reference 900 km disposal orbit reaches approximately 899.4 × 900.6 km using 165.5 kg. The fixed South Pacific target has a nominal error of approximately 28 km using 122.0 kg. The conditional reference-body ellipse has 95% semiaxes of about 427 km and 13 km. These checks assess the prescribed terminal dynamics even when a separate component thermal qualification fails earlier.

## Attitude, articulated power and heat

The mission inventory carries an inertial attitude quaternion, angular rates, wing and radiator encoders, and steerable terminal boresights. Scheduled maneuver preparation, unloading, terminal acquisition and mission phases select the guidance reference. A snapshot exposes both the commanded mode and the achieved pose. Random-access playback interpolates stored body poses and resolves terminal pursuit of the moving Earth station from the previous checkpoint. A geometric contact alone does not admit Ka or optical data. The terminal must complete acquisition and lie inside its travel cone and half-beamwidth residual. The optical receiver additionally incurs a separate jitter loss. Relay service has an assumed availability schedule without a receiver ephemeris.

Two radiator roots open outward to a width-dependent clearance angle (45° to 85°). After both outer hinges lock, independent span-axis drives trim within ±105° at up to 0.75°/s. The clearance interlock narrows the upper bank’s cant if the rotary sweep could enter the moving terminal envelopes. Each bank’s trim limit also keeps its sheet within its own side of the bank pair. When these limits prevent edge-on pointing, the thermal balance includes the remaining illumination. Sunlit tracking seeks zero direct solar projection. In eclipse a warm loop minimizes Earth view and a cold loop maximizes it. Cold protection starts 8 K above the higher of the loop and radiator operating minima and releases 5 K warmer. These rates, margins, and the two-axis installation are study choices. Before a chemical burn trim returns to zero before cant returns to ±45°.

Power uses the positive cosine of the achieved photovoltaic normal and solar vector. Heat rejection uses both sides of each wetted bank. Direct sunlight heats the Sun-facing side. For planetary loads the solver integrates the absolute projected cosine over the finite Earth disk and divides by π. The corresponding two-face disk factor ranges from 0 to 1. Averaging over the two emitting faces gives an open-space fraction of one minus half this factor. Planetary radiance uses 237 W/m² infrared and an illustrative 0.30 diffuse albedo scaled by the local solar zenith cosine. The closed-form disk integral includes the visible planetary limb for a bank pointing edge-on to Earth. Self-shadowing and panel-to-panel radiation are omitted. Direct, reflected and infrared terms add to absorbed power. The same procedure area-weights the opposing enclosure faces. Battery, radiator/coolant and chip temperatures are advanced on the mission clock with drive power included.

The tracking drive allocation is 12 W plus 24 W per degree/s of the summed absolute representative wing and two radiator-head rates, with the summed rate capped at 2°/s. The allocation describes all four installed wing drives and both radiator drives together. Steerable terminal motion adds 6 W per degree/s, capped at 4°/s. These are engineering assumptions. Motors follow their nominal command profile under the electrical regulation assumption. A hard brownout and subsequent attitude-control recovery are outside the model.

After deployment, the drag model caches exact projected unions of the bus, four wing blankets and two radiator banks in nearest 5° bins for the ram direction and three encoder angles. Gravity-gradient torque uses the current radial vector and a rectangular bus inertia approximation. Reported scalar CMG demand includes maneuver and antenna momentum plus carried disturbance accumulation. Chemical ignition checks the actual thrust-axis alignment, configuration-dependent mass and plume clearance, and angular authority to follow the rotating velocity direction. Once admitted, the burn trajectory assumes nominal velocity-vector tracking. The raised-orbit second ignition is screened independently.

Flight reference material and the guidance equations are recorded in `dist/attitude-guidance.js`. NASA's environmental heat formulation provides the one-face nadir disk-factor cross-check and reflected-flux basis at https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20160012100/downloads/20160012100.pdf.
