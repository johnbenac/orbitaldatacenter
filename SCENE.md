scene.js exports createScene(container, {onPick:(componentId)=>{}}={}) synchronously. It returns {update(hardware, snapshot), setView(view), setOverlays(listOfIds), highlight(id), resetCamera(), destroy()}. view can be 'spacecraft','orbit','core' or displayed labels. Overlay IDs sun, albedo, ir, radiator, ram, pressure, thrust, dimensions. Component IDs solar,radiator,compute,storage,battery,propellant,cmg,communications,propulsion,structure. Selection invokes onPick with a component ID. The container must have position:relative, an explicit surface height, and the scene fills its bounds absolutely. Overlay controls/buttons and externally rendered annotation text are owned by the root UI.

The static client vendors Three.js r160, licensed under MIT (dist/vendor/THREE-LICENSE.txt). The Earth surface is NASA Blue Marble imagery distributed in the Three.js examples at https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r128/examples/textures/planets/earth_atmos_2048.jpg. The close-up orbital limb uses an orthographic remap of that same image with a procedural atmosphere and seeded starfield. The global view uses the texture on the Earth sphere with actual simulation geometry. The close-up backdrop is illustrative.

Solar panels use three hinged leaves per wing. The spacecraft camera tracks the vehicle attitude. ECI solar illumination, body basis, radiator faces and instantaneous ground-terminal bearing use the physics snapshot. Ground contacts draw to the Earth station at orbital scale. The close-up truncates the beam at the camera extent. A local textured software triangle renderer uses the same Three.js meshes when WebGL is unavailable.

## Launch, enclosure, and drawing verification

The fourth camera key is `launch`. The scene accepts `setChapter(id)` and an `onHover(id, clientX, clientY)` callback. The launch configuration follows the shared flight phase and time, including negative elapsed time on the ground. Outside the launch and disposal episodes it displays a labeled, stowed design reference. The five launcher profiles and the dimensioned cassette sections come from the launch sizing model. Fairing shells, stage proportions, staging cues, launch buildings, service equipment, handling rig and atmospheric background are illustrative. The Starship scene retains the bay on the upper stage. The Ground Integration and Pad scenes include procedural, locally generated scenery and engineering handling fixtures.

The operational vehicle has six finite-thickness enclosure walls with explicit round apertures. Occupied sleeves and gasket flanges are dimensioned from the enclosure inventory. The positive-axis side, deck and bulkhead are removed only for the Compute Core preset. The three other walls and the rack mounts remain visible. The radiator banks and solar wings carry individually animated rigid leaves. They translate from the launch cassette and unfold at their drives. The compute cutaway shows one installed row per tray through 28 trays. Larger designs show 28 representative rows with an explicit on-screen count.

Run `node mesh-checks.mjs` to check scale handedness, finite vertex coordinates, face winding against mesh normals, zero-area triangles, manifold edge parity, positive enclosed volume, ray hits on all six exterior walls, occupied penetrations, chapter framing and tray representation. The software renderer uses the same Three.js buffers and normal matrices, interpolated lighting, perspective-correct texture sampling, mipmaps, alpha and antialiasing. GPU launch scenes additionally cast shadows from the installed hardware and handling fixtures.

## Temperature layer and camera controls

Pass `thermal` to `scene.setOverlays()` for the live temperature field. The six mapped nodes are chip junction, cold plate, coolant loop, radiator face, battery pack and exterior blanket. Color uses the spacecraft's current `thermalQualification` records and one of two fixed scales. Working spans 245–305 K and is selected initially. Full spans 180–380 K. The endpoints saturate while the exact model temperatures remain available in the readout. The legend counts assessed nodes beyond those endpoints. Neither scale changes with the clock. White diagonal hatching denotes a cold or hot excursion against the active band's full-precision limits. Gray represents hardware without a live temperature node. The stowed operational reference and the Earth-scale camera point visitors to the vehicle views. Ground-integration and launch visuals can show the modeled payload skin and stowed radiator under the prescribed thermal boundary.

`onHover(part, clientX, clientY, qualification)` and `onPick(part, qualification)` receive the current mesh's qualification record or null. `setLegendVisible(boolean)` controls the in-view key. Use `setThermalScale('working'|'full')` and `getThermalScale()` to restore or save its range. Choosing a range in the legend fires the optional `onThermalScale(id)` callback. Left drag orbits, middle drag pans, and the wheel zooms. Two contacts pan and pinch. Shift + arrow keys pan; Home resets the current preset. `getCameraState()` and `setCameraState(states)` round-trip one [azimuth, elevation, zoom, panX, panY, panZ] tuple per camera. Panning follows camera right/up at the current focus distance. Each local pan offset survives camera switches and viewport resizes.

Run `node scene-interaction-checks.mjs` to exercise material mapping and restoration, band hatching, the common palette, neutral hardware and the four cameras' pan, zoom, reset and serialization paths.
