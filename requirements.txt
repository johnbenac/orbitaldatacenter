DEVELOPER SPECIFICATION

1. User controls and presets

Provide two explicit operations. Size the design changes hardware dimensions for the chosen capacity or constraint. Fly the design keeps component counts, installed dimensions, dry masses and tank capacities fixed as attitude, temperatures, energy, data and propellant evolve. Allow compute tray count, storage or battery module count, surface area, aperture, thermal capacity, tank volume and actuator ratings to scale within the reference architecture. Keep the wing count, hinges, topology, tank/engine plumbing and locations of mounts under developer control. The user can command an installed joint within its actual limits. Show a deployed and a stowed drawing of the developer’s baseline.

Offer enumerated presets for compute and workload profiles, storage devices, battery chemistry, PV material or cell technology, radiator coating, compatible chemical propellant/engine packages, launch vehicle, and ground network. Mark preset parameters and user overrides. Choosing a preset populates a consistent bundle of editable assumptions with units, valid ranges, sources, dates and scope. For example, a cell chemistry populates pack-level Wh/kg, power limits and aging assumptions; an engine/propellant pair populates thrust, delivered specific impulse, mixture ratio, fluid densities and tank/feed assumptions. Validate overrides for compatibility. Selecting a different propellant never silently changes the number or purpose of the tanks. Material and chemistry presets can size interchangeable hardware within its defined mounting and feed interfaces. When solving for a categorical variable, enumerate all eligible choices and headroom. Identify ties or missing inputs. Continuous inverse solves use bounded physical ranges; sizing solves return feasible whole units and achieved capacity.

Mission duration is a solve target as well as an input. An opening design may specify 10 MW IT load and a 550 km circular orbit that passes through shadow. Open with duration solved from installed hardware and supplies, or let the user enter it. In lifetime-sizing mode, install the resources needed for the user-selected duration. In endurance mode hold hardware, propellant, operating policy and degradation laws fixed; find the first nominal service, energy, temperature, momentum, propellant, or other modeled limit reached. Report when it occurs, the active constraint, and the remaining margins. If the model finds no limit in its numerical search horizon, display the lower bound with that horizon. Show a beginning-of-life infeasibility at elapsed time zero. Keep coverage years for station-keeping propellant distinct from required service duration. Give fast live results during drags and identify any preview before displaying a resolved mission sweep.

2. Time, orbit, attitude and environment

Support all six classical elements at an epoch: semi-major axis, eccentricity, inclination, right ascension of the ascending node, argument of periapsis, and mean anomaly at epoch. Give users orbital position/playback and controls for all applicable elements. Handle circular/equatorial degeneracies, reject Earth-intersecting starting orbits and show derived altitudes, period, inclination, sunlight fraction and signed solar beta angle (angle of the Sun above or below the orbital plane). Use time-correct Kepler propagation for orbit playback, Sun and shadow geometry in a common frame, and a documented treatment of seasonal lighting, secular orbital-plane precession, drag, atmospheric-relative velocity, finite burns and spacecraft mass for multi-year analysis. Show the dawn-dusk geometry and eclipse seasons when applicable; a 24-hour window can have zero eclipses. Compute seconds in eclipse, each entry and exit, and battery reserve at the deepest discharge; avoid a fixed eclipse fraction.

Implement solar-cycle-driven thermospheric drag with a documented model such as NRLMSIS or a validated lookup table. Use altitude, orbital position, epoch, solar activity (daily and 81-day F10.7) and geomagnetic activity (Ap), with model validity indicated. Offer replay of an observed cycle and editable nominal cycle envelopes/phase over roughly eleven years. Keep the F10.7 density driver distinct from solar irradiance powering the arrays. The mission window can cover a fraction of a cycle or multiple cycles. Compute exposed projected area from the silhouette normal to relative atmospheric flow, handling overlaps and articulated surfaces consistently. Per surface or equivalent summed model: D = ½ρ v_rel² C_D A_ram. Show ballistic coefficient, drag force, acceleration, integrated drag impulse and propellant demand. Changing ram attitude can reduce drag while reducing available solar power or net cooling. Distinguish this from the positive work done during a prograde chemical burn. If optical radiation pressure is modeled, identify its own force, torque, eclipse behavior and signed orbital work.

In spacecraft view, show separately selectable direct solar rays, Earth-reflected shortwave light, Earth infrared, radiator emission, ram, pressure forces and thrust. Use direction, labels and line style as well as color; distinguish force (N), torque (N·m), irradiance (W/m²), heat flow (W), data (bit/s), and energy (Wh). Selecting a surface shows its normal, Sun and ram incidence, Earth view factor and current contribution. Size photovoltaic and radiator wings in metres from area and the fixed geometry; hold camera framing steady during slider drags.

3. Shared subsystem workspaces

Use a legible 2D chart or ledger as the main display in every subsystem workspace. Put event markers on the same time axis and make a click jump to that event in a relevant 3D camera. A 24-hour window means 86,400 consecutive seconds starting at any selected mission instant where that interval fits. A mission timeline begins at launch, extends to the input or solved endpoint and distinguishes commissioning from useful service time. Show both the date/elapsed age and orbit number. Use energy-, impulse- and byte-conserving integrations; carry SOC, temperatures, queued data, momentum, and propellant continuously across windows. Use event-aware sampling. Aggregated mission charts retain extrema and uncertainty where a coarse model is used. For the launch event, also supply seconds/minutes on a dedicated insertion timeline.

Compute & Storage. Include a service summary with current and sustainable input-prefill tokens/s, output-decode tokens/s per stream and aggregate, concurrent sessions, time to first token, request throughput, utilization, peak/sustained FLOP/s at named precision, IT kW, and tokens per joule for a stated electrical boundary. Servers, storage, hosts, switches, pumps, and interfaces each have a declared mass/power boundary. Account for throughput limits, request backlog, storage backpressure and full/empty queues.

Communications. The reference layout uses opposing body-mounted S-band patches for command/telemetry uplink and downlink, a steerable high-gain Ka-band reflector for routine bulk downlink, and a fine-steered optical telescope (e.g. 1,550 nm) for large transfers. Document each band/center frequency or wavelength, aperture, gain, divergence/beamwidth, pointing limits and intended service. Show use of the least demanding installed link that meets a traffic case. Use a high-rate upload path only where a matching spacecraft receiver and ground transmitter are explicitly budgeted; uplink and downlink have separate rates. The site should reveal when RF suffices for serving text versus when moving weights, imagery or checkpoints motivates optical bandwidth.
  • RF: derive achievable coded rate from transmitter output, gain/aperture, frequency, range, atmospheric and pointing losses, receiver sensitivity or G/T, bandwidth, and coding requirements. Show link margin. Optical: use transmitted optical power, aperture/divergence, receiver aperture and sensitivity, pointing/jitter, atmosphere, clear-sky availability and coding. Show terminal electrical draw, cooling, slew/acquisition time, and ground endpoint. Distinguish a deterministic contact schedule from expected availability based on climatology.
  • Compute Earth blockage, actual slant range, ground station visibility and elevation masks, scheduled access, terminal travel, pointing error and acquisition overhead. Show the transmitter, receiver, on-Earth footprint and acquisition cone. Indicate when rendered beam width is enlarged for legibility. End the beam when a contact ends and continue growing the queue according to the workload.
  • Plot physical bit rate, application goodput, bytes waiting, GB per pass, total uplink/downlink per 24 hours, and mission totals. Link output tokens to bytes using the specified tokenizer/workload or an exposed serialized-bytes-per-token assumption. Budget protocol overhead, model uploads, logs and bulk payloads separately. Cap deliveries at queued bytes plus contemporaneous arrivals; interrupted uploads can resume where the chosen transfer policy permits. Make offered workload, disk, buffers, link capacity and on-time delivery independently inspectable.

Battery & Power. Include a visible battery pack and a pack-level ledger. Track physical mass in kg, volume, installed nameplate kWh, age-adjusted available capacity, usable operational capacity between SOC limits, stored kWh, SOC %, charge/discharge kW caps, temperature, SOC minimum, cumulative cycles, throughput and charge/discharge efficiency. Derive pack mass from pack-level specific energy and honor peak power and temperature constraints; expose pack overhead if using cell-level values. Keep battery mass fixed as it cycles. Compute charge or discharge from generation minus actual bus demand, using dE/dt = η_charge P_charge − P_discharge/η_discharge with bus-side power conventions. Plot solar generation versus itemized loads (IT, storage, communications, pumps, CMGs, joints, magnetorquers, heaters, propulsion auxiliaries), charge/discharge, curtailed power, unmet load, and SOC in kWh and %. Show instantaneous charge/discharge, highest load, lowest SOC and timing. For a repeating design orbit/day verify recharge without drawing down the initial energy over cycles. For seasonal operation carry energy across actual consecutive days.

Thermal. Make a heat-flow diagram and a time plot from chip junction through package, cold plate/heat pipe, coolant, radiator and space. Track m·c_p, junction limit, thermal resistance, coolant flow, pump power, temperatures, incident heat, gross emission, net cooling capacity, actual rejection, stored heat and remaining margin. Use C_i = m_i c_p,i, conduction (T_i−T_j)/R_ij, and C_i dT_i/dt = net heat flow. For a pumped loop use Q = ṁ c_p ΔT. Size for the cold-plate and junction limits as well as radiator area; active temperature lift, if modeled, draws power and adds heat. Link onboard consumed electricity and modeled onboard losses to heat exactly once, accounting for energy exported in signals or deposited elsewhere when significant. Provide an independent applied-thermal-load mode for sensitivity experiments, clearly labeled. Per radiator face, a diffuse approximation is q_net = εσT⁴ − α(G_sun + G_albedo) − εG_EarthIR in W/m². Determine albedo from the sunlit visible Earth; Earth infrared persists in eclipse. Use the actual face orientation, emission from active faces, and documented self-view/shadow assumptions; distinguish physical panel area from total emitting area. Solar wings dissipate their own absorbed heat according to the stated thermal boundary.

Attitude Control. Pick and draw a fixed CMG cluster (for example a four-unit pyramid), its mounted axes, sensors, gimbals and magnetic unloading actuators. Recompute center of mass, center of pressure and inertia as appendages deploy or rotate and propellant decreases. Compute drag torque from Σ[(r_CP,i−r_CM)×F_i]. Include solar-pressure and gravity-gradient torques and commanded slew/deployment momentum where material. Compare commanded body torque and integrated disturbance momentum with direction-dependent CMG capability, gimbal travel, slew rates and steering-law limits, including loss of authority near a singularity. A reduced-order model must state its envelope. Use sourced or editable motor maps/estimates for spin maintenance, gimbal drive, electronics, nominal slews and magnetic coils. Power must reflect actuator operation, spin-up and losses, even during steady pointing. Plot torque by source and axis (N·m), momentum (N·m·s), margin, pointing error/jitter and power (W). Show a nominal desaturation trigger and schedule based on momentum envelope/direction. Magnetic unloading torque is m_dipole × B, limited by the changing local magnetic field, dipole and duty constraints. Model the external angular impulse, CMG response and electrical heating; this nominal unloading consumes zero chemical propellant. If available magnetic authority cannot support the desired schedule or pointing, return the margin/shortfall. Share the pointing history with the antenna link and the solar and thermal models.

Propellant. Use a pressure-fed, storable chemical system as the reference (e.g. a bipropellant layout). Expose a delivered-performance fraction when using ideal I_sp; apply it once. The rocket supplies jet power from propellants. NUMERICAL AND INTERACTION CHECKS

1. At 1,361 W/m², full normal sunshine, 30% PV efficiency and no other losses, 1 MW requires 2,449 m² to rounding. A 60° tilt halves direct output. In full shadow direct output is zero. In flight, installed surfaces retain their dimensions. Doubling panel area at fixed aspect ratio and count multiplies both dimensions by √2.
2. With zero incoming flux, 300 K, emissivity 0.90, and one emitting face, 1 MW needs approximately 2,419 m² of radiator. Two equally exposed active faces halve the required physical planform area. If coating ε goes from 0.90 to 0.81, gross emission under otherwise fixed conditions declines 10%. A 10 kg thermal node with c_p = 500 J/(kg·K) and net inflow of 1 kW initially warms at 0.20 K/s. Verify separate transport and radiator limits.
3. A battery initially holding 20 kWh that supplies 9 kW at its bus for one hour at 90% discharge efficiency ends at 10 kWh, within operating limits. A pack initially holding 10 kWh receiving 10 kWh bus energy at 90% charge efficiency reaches 19 kWh. Pack mass stays fixed. A full battery must curtail excess supply; an empty usable store must flag unmet demand. A 100 kWh pack at an assumed pack-level 150 Wh/kg weighs about 667 kg before items explicitly outside that boundary.
4. At a defined serialized payload of four bytes per output token, 10,000 output tokens/s creates 0.32 Mbit/s before overhead. A 600 s downlink at 1 Gbit/s net can deliver at most 75 decimal GB, equivalent to about 6.94 Mbit/s averaged over 24 hours. If only 20 GB are queued with no arrivals, only 20 GB are delivered. Test the distinct input/output, memory and disk boundaries and bidirectional service from an intermittent contact schedule. A 9 Tbit recorder holds 1.125 decimal TB before reserves.
5. At ρ = 10⁻¹² kg/m³, v_rel = 7,500 m/s, C_D = 2 and exposed area = 1,000 m², drag is 0.05625 N. With a perpendicular 2 m center-of-pressure lever arm it produces 0.1125 N·m. Put the pressure center at the mass center: the aerodynamic torque vanishes while drag and required reboost impulse remain. With a magnetic dipole parallel to B, unloading torque is zero. A CMG-only slew or magnetic unload expends electrical energy and zero chemical propellant.
6. At wet mass 10,000 kg, delivered I_sp = 300 s and ideal Δv = 10 m/s, chemical propellant use is about 33.93 kg. For a 2:1 oxidizer-to-fuel mass ratio, about 22.62 kg is oxidizer and 11.31 kg is fuel. At fixed delivered I_sp and Δv, avionics efficiency alone cannot change the ideal rocket-equation mass. Check the visual and both ledgers against the burn record. Deployment conserves mass absent a documented jettison; launcher ascent does not empty spacecraft tanks.
7. For a synthetic chip at 100 TFLOP/s peak and 50% active workload efficiency, 510 TFLOP/s requires 11 simultaneous chips and yields 550 TFLOP/s. Half-time operation yields 275 TFLOP/s averaged over time and still needs hardware for the active interval. Check the achievable throughput returned by each inverse solve.
8. Given constant annual fractional PV retention r, after t years generation in the same geometry is P_BOL × r^t. Changing requested mission duration leaves performance at an already chosen earlier age unchanged. Zero degradation makes matching BOL and later conditions produce matching performance. Increased age must not magically replenish charge, cooling margin, propellant or storage endurance. Check all-sun and eclipsing 24-hour scenarios, a full-cycle mission sweep, and both directions of the lifetime solve.
9. A 25 t launch stack versus a 23.5 t reference has a 1.5 t shortfall before orbit-specific adjustments. Changing technology data must recompute mass, geometry and downstream demands. Changing screens must not change a physical result. Reload a shared link mid-burn and midway through a ground pass; validate the selected instant, tank quantities, SOC, temperatures, queues, deployed configuration and selected view against the original.
