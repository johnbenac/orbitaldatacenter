scene.js exports createScene(container, {onPick:(componentId)=>{}}={}) synchronously. It returns {update(hardware, snapshot), setView(view), setOverlays(listOfIds), highlight(id), resetCamera(), destroy()}. view can be 'spacecraft','orbit','core' or displayed labels. Overlay IDs sun, albedo, ir, radiator, ram, pressure, thrust, dimensions. Component IDs solar,radiator,compute,storage,battery,propellant,cmg,communications,propulsion,structure. Selection invokes onPick with a component ID. The container must have position:relative, an explicit surface height, and the scene fills its bounds absolutely. Overlay controls/buttons and externally rendered annotation text are owned by the root UI.

The static client vendors Three.js r160, licensed under MIT (dist/vendor/THREE-LICENSE.txt). The Earth surface is NASA Blue Marble imagery distributed in the Three.js examples at https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r128/examples/textures/planets/earth_atmos_2048.jpg. The close-up orbital limb uses an orthographic remap of that same image with a procedural atmosphere and seeded starfield. The global view uses the texture on the Earth sphere with actual simulation geometry. The close-up backdrop is illustrative.

Solar panels use three hinged leaves per wing. The spacecraft camera tracks the vehicle attitude. ECI solar illumination, body basis, radiator faces and instantaneous ground-terminal bearing use the physics snapshot. Ground contacts draw to the Earth station at orbital scale. The close-up truncates the beam at the camera extent. A local textured software triangle renderer uses the same Three.js meshes when WebGL is unavailable.
